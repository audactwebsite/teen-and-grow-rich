# Review 2 — "Leave with proof"

Received 2 August 2026. A strategy document rather than a site audit, and the reviewer says
so up front:

> *"Ik kan hier de live website niet technisch crawlen/pixel-checken … dus ik ga niet doen
> alsof ik elke knop op de site heb getest."*

That is to their credit and it explains the shape of what follows: a large number of
proposals describe things that already exist, because they were written for a site the
author had not seen. Everything checkable below was measured before deciding.

**Two findings are genuinely new and both are worth building.** One of them confirms a
pattern that review 1 also hit.

---

## The pattern, now confirmed twice

Review 1 found that *"Your future is not built by how inspired you feel while reading. It
is built by what exists after you close the book."* appears nowhere on this site.

This review independently lands on a different sentence and calls it the brand soul:

> **START TINY. STAY HONEST. BUILD RECEIPTS.**

Measured: printed page 62, set in capitals as the last line of the Afterword. **Also
nowhere on the site.** So is *"from consuming to creating"*, printed page 63.

Two reviewers, working from the book, independently picked out the lines that carry the
thesis — and every one of them is missing. That is not two coincidences. It is a structural
gap in the extraction: **the pipeline pulls the book's structure and skips the book's
voice.** Chapters, builds, Reality Checks, worksheet fields, dated actions and offers are
all in. The Note From Ryan, the Afterword's closing line and the author's own framing are
not.

**Adopt.** Extracting Ryan's voice is now the first item across all reviews, with an
assertion so it cannot silently drop again.

---

## Adopt — new

### 1. The Safe Witness Loop

The best structural idea in this review, and it survives every rule this project has.

The book leans on the "safe witness" throughout — a parent, teacher, coach or mentor who
keeps you honest. Measured: the phrase appears on 8 pages, and 25 pages tell a reader to
involve an adult. **But it exists only as a field to fill in.** Nothing on this site helps a
thirteen-year-old actually ask that person.

The proposal is a generated message the teen sends themselves:

> *I'm starting a 30-day build from Teen & Grow Rich. My goal is ___. My proof project is
> ___. My stop rules are ___. Can I show you progress once a week, and ask for help if
> money, accounts, posting or clients are involved?*

Why it fits: it is text, generated in the browser from what the reader has already typed
into a worksheet, copied by the reader, sent by the reader. Nothing leaves the device,
nothing is stored, no address is collected. The copy-as-text machinery already exists.

And the second-order effect the reviewer names is real: **the adult arrives via the child,
not via an advertisement.** That is the opposite of a marketing funnel and it is better
than one.

### 2. Route from the lowest Real Rich area to the chapter that covers it

The scoreboard already does most of what the "Real Rich Score quiz" proposes: six areas,
1–10, and it computes and names your lowest. It already refuses to total them — the code
carries the reason, *"a total would be a grade, and the book's instruction is to pick one
action for the lowest area"*, which is the same argument that killed review 1's Real Rich
Index.

What is missing is one step: from *"your lowest is Skills"* to *"chapter 4, Level Up, and
here is its 24-Hour Build"*. Small change, real payoff.

### 3. Rubrics on the teachers page

Schools do not buy motivation, they buy material they can assess. The review is right that
a rubric — problem clarity, skill practice, evidence, acting on feedback, safety decisions,
reflection — maps almost exactly onto what a receipt already is. New, and useful.

### 4. Domain variants

Measured at the registrar level: `teen-and-grow-rich.com`, `teenandgrowrich.nl` and
`teen-andgrowrich.nl` all resolve to nothing and appear unregistered. The hyphen-free
`.com` is the one that belongs to somebody else and is not obtainable.

Worth registering the near-misses and pointing them at the real site — a reader typing the
address from memory is exactly the reader this project cannot afford to lose. Owner's call
and owner's money, but the check is done.

## Where the review contradicts itself, usefully

It proposes measuring nine funnel metrics *and* says the north star should be "number of
receipts created". Those cannot both happen here: counting receipts means watching what a
reader does on their own device.

The resolution is the one the site already made. The receipt count is real and it is the
reader's — shown to them on `/receipts`, printed on their folder index, and never
transmitted. The project's claim is not that it can measure agency. It is that agency
leaves evidence, and the evidence belongs to the person who made it.

That is a stronger position than the funnel, and it is the review's own argument taken one
step further than the review took it.
