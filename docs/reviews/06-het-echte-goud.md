# Review 6 — "Het echte goud"

Received 2 August 2026. Two passes: a strategy analysis, then a much deeper second pass
after the reviewer was told to dig further. The second pass is the valuable one and it was
cut off mid-sentence in transmission — everything below layer 4 is missing and has not been
read.

It is the most ambitious review of the seven and the one most willing to be wrong in
interesting ways. **It also, without meaning to, produces the hardest possible proof of
review 1's finding.**

---

## The accidental finding, which is the most important thing in this review

The review nominates one sentence as the best line in the whole project and says it belongs
in the homepage hero:

> *"A stopwatch does not care how you felt about it, whether you meant to, or what you
> posted afterwards. Either the lap was quicker or it was not."*

It calls this **"Ryan's beste marketingregel"** — Ryan's best line.

Measured: it is at [`src/pages/proof.astro:269`](../../src/pages/proof.astro#L269). **It is
not Ryan's. It is ours.** It is site editorial, written for `/proof`, and it appears nowhere
in the book.

Review 1 found that Ryan's own voice is entirely absent from this site — that "A Note From
Ryan" on printed page 3 was never extracted, and that every word here is either the book's
structural content or our own editorial. This review is the consequence, demonstrated
live: **a careful reader who read the whole site could not tell the site's voice from the
author's, and picked ours.**

That is worse than the gap being noticed. It means the gap is already being filled by us,
invisibly, in a project whose entire claim is peer authenticity.

**No change to the priority — extracting Ryan's voice was already first — but this is the
argument that should be attached to it.** And the stopwatch line, whatever its quality,
cannot go in the hero. Putting our sentence where the author's belongs is precisely the
substitution that just got caught.

## Adopt

### 1. The Hill audit is misfiled

The review says `/hill` is buried and a homepage visitor never reaches it. **Half wrong:**
it is on the homepage, at [`index.astro:414`](../../src/pages/index.astro#L414), as the
fourth of five doors.

But the section those doors sit in is headed **"For the adults"**. So the sharpest thing
this site says — *the man who wrote the book that inspired this one ran a fake college, was
named in Illinois blue-sky warrants and drew an FTC complaint, and the Carnegie meeting has
no evidence behind it* — is currently filed next to the privacy policy, as an adult
concern.

It is not an adult concern. It is the most interesting page on the site to a
thirteen-year-old, and it is the one page that demonstrates the method instead of
describing it. Filing it under compliance is a real mistake and no other review caught it.

**Adopt:** the Hill audit gets its own place, aimed at the reader, not a slot in the
adults' list.

### 2. The traffic-light rule is not a money framework

The review's sharpest structural point:

> Het is direct toepasbaar op dating, drugs, sociale media, reizen, delen van locatie,
> contracten, en online contact. Dit is in wezen een public-health primitive vermomd als
> money-book concept.

That is true and the site does not say it. `/race-control` presents green/yellow/red inside
a money-and-building context because that is the book's context. But the rule itself is
about *reversibility and who else is affected*, which has nothing to do with money — and
the book already knows this: 25 pages tell a reader to involve an adult, across topics that
are not financial at all.

Widening the framing on `/race-control` costs nothing, is true to the book, and makes the
page useful to a school that teaches no financial education at all.

**Adopt.** It also makes the traffic-light poster from review 3 a much better object.

### 3. SEO, reframed — and this is materially different from review 2's version

Review 2 proposed eleven SEO article pages. Rejected: a content programme nobody has
committed to writing.

This review lists the same idea but the examples change the argument entirely:

- *Is Napoleon Hill's biography true?* → `/hill` already answers this, with sources, better
  than anything else on the web
- *Family agreement template for teens working online* → `/parents` already has it, printable
- *Green yellow red traffic light rule teens* → `/race-control` already is it

**These pages exist.** They are simply not titled or described as the answer to the
question a person actually types. That is a metadata and heading job on pages already
built, not a content programme — and it is the only growth route available to a site with
no analytics and no ad budget.

**Adopt.** Cheap, honest, no new content, no tricks.

### 4. Legal review of the money chapter

Nobody else raised this. Chapter 12 carries a Spend / Save / Build split, and the site
renders the book's wording verbatim. The book contains no income promises and no investment
advice, which is the important part — but "verbatim from the book" is not a defence if the
book itself has a grey edge.

A single read by someone who works in child law and financial-promotion rules, in NL and
the two largest English-speaking markets, is small money against a real tail risk.

**Adopt to Ryan's list.**

### 5. Retailer routes, named

`/get` says "Retailer link pending" and it is the oldest open item. The review supplies
concrete routes rather than restating the problem: Bookshop.org for UK/EU, Amazon KDP
paperback as the default English search surface, **Centraal Boekhuis** for NL/BE library and
bookshop distribution, and a direct route for the "I want thirty for my class" flow that
schools actually use.

Not a site change. **Useful information for the owner**, and the Centraal Boekhuis point in
particular is the kind of thing only somebody who knows the Dutch trade would say.

### 6. brightkids.ai says twelve — now confirmed by a second reader

Review 3 raised this and could not verify it; the fetch failed. This review reports the same
thing independently: *"Ryan is de 12-jarige oprichter van BrightKids.ai"*.

Our side was measured already: this site says thirteen consistently, and all eight
occurrences of "twelve" come from the book's own stories, none of them an age.

Two reviewers, one of whom read the page. **Escalated on the owner's list** — on a site
whose claim is that every statement is checkable, two of Ryan's own properties disagreeing
about his age is the cheapest possible thing to get wrong.

## The framing worth keeping

### Post-authority self-help (layer 1)

The argument: the genre runs on borrowed authority — Hill's Carnegie meeting that has no
evidence, a rich dad who was probably fictional — and the reader is buying access to
something the author claims to know. Strip the authority claims out and only the **method**
survives. Pick one thing, make it smaller, do it by tomorrow night, keep the proof.

That is an accurate description of what `/hill` does to this project's own inspiration, and
it is better language than anything in the docs.

**One limit.** The review builds the case by naming living authors and asserting their
claims are false. `/hill` works because Hill's 1908–1919 record sits in court filings and
FTC documents that can be linked. "Robbins claims neuroscience that is not there" meets no
such standard. **Adopt the framing, drop the roster** — this site does not get to make
unsourced accusations, least of all in the section explaining why it does not make unsourced
claims.

### Only a thirteen-year-old could write this (layer 2)

> Een 40-jarige zelfhulpauteur kan dit boek niet schrijven. Hij zou onmiddellijk beschuldigd
> worden van hypocrisie — waar zijn zijn resultaten? Een 13-jarige die datzelfde zegt is
> onaanvechtbaar.

Review 3 said *age is a receipt, not a claim*, and that the changelog becomes an
autobiography. This one sharpens it: the credibility structure itself depends on the age,
and it **expires around fifteen or sixteen** — not because Ryan gets less interesting, but
because the argument stops being unfalsifiable once he has visible success.

Both are right and they point at the same thing. `/updates` being append-only and dated
already handles it structurally. Worth having in writing so nobody later "refreshes" the
framing.

### Receipts is forensics, not mindset (layer 4)

The sharpest idea in the review:

> Receipts beats motivation is niet mindset-advies. Het is forensisch-boekhoudkundige
> denkkunst toegepast op het eigen leven.

Double-entry bookkeeping, evidentiary standards, reproducibility, source verification — not
the self-help tradition the book is shelved in. Which means `/hill`, the primary-source rule
and the append-only changelog are not compliance pages bolted onto a motivation book. **They
are the genre.**

That is the same observation review 3 made from the other end — *the site uses its own
method on itself* — and having two independent readers land on it means it should be said
out loud somewhere on the site rather than only being true.

### The framework as policy material (layer 3)

The claim: the traffic-light rule plus 19 Reality Checks plus the family agreement amount to
a teen digital-safety framework more operationalised than what regulators produce, at a
moment when the EU AI Act, the UK Online Safety Act, KOSA and the Australian under-16 rules
are all reaching for exactly that.

Whether that is true is not ours to decide and the budget figures quoted are unsourced. But
it has one site-side consequence worth acting on: if that audience is real, the framework
needs to exist **as a framework** on one page, not only as chapter content. `/race-control`
plus adoption 2 above is most of the way there, and it serves teachers regardless of whether
a single regulator ever reads it.

## Not adopting

### The receipts wall — third proposal, and this is the standing answer

Review 1 proposed it. Review 4 proposed it as a "viral proof loop". This one proposes the
most careful version yet: adult-vetted, first-name-only, one per week, **submitted by email
to the existing monitored inbox rather than a form**, so *"nowhere to type your email"*
stays literally true.

The submission mechanism is genuinely solved. It still fails, for two reasons that no
mechanism fixes:

1. **It is user-submitted content about minors.** Non-negotiable 5. Moderating it is the
   liability this project will not take on, and doing it by email does not make the
   moderation go away — it just moves it into somebody's inbox.
2. **The book argues against it on the page it would sit next to.** Chapter 1's Reality
   Check: *"You do not have to post your dream publicly. Accountability can be private,
   especially when safety, family, or anxiety matters. The goal is action, not exposure."*

A wall of other teenagers' finished work is a comparison surface whatever the submission
route. **This is now the settled answer and it does not need re-arguing.**

### Sponsored print runs — second proposal, same answer

Review 4 answered this. A bank funding free books for schools is still influence on a page a
thirteen-year-old reads, whether or not a word changes. The version that survives is
unchanged: **a school or a foundation buys books at full price and gives them away.** That
is a customer, not a sponsor — no logo, no placement, no editorial contact, and it appears
in the changelog as a sale.

### Demoting the page-number field

> De bookpage-lookup mag blijven, maar één schuif naar beneden — hij is een utility, geen entree.

No, and the reasoning is inverted. **Neither printed QR code deep-links.** There are exactly
two of them and both encode the bare root `https://teen-andgrowrich.com` — verified by
rendering every page of the print files and running QR detection. So a scan made on page 41
lands on the homepage, and the reader is still holding a page whose tool they now have to go
find. The page-number field is what closes that gap; it is the entrance for a book-holder,
not a leftover.

It also works where a camera cannot. Dutch classrooms have banned phones, tablets and
smartwatches since 1 January 2024, and school Chromebooks have no camera workflow — a
typeable number works on a locked-down device, on a projector, and read aloud by a teacher.
The reader most likely to be holding this book in the country it was written in is the
reader who cannot scan anything.

`/p/1`–`/p/65` is therefore not a utility. It is the *only route from a printed page to that
page's tool*, for every copy of the first print run, forever. It stays first.

(An earlier version of this entry said both codes encode the hyphen-free domain and send
readers to a stranger's website. That was wrong: the book was re-issued precisely to fix the
domain, and the re-issued files are what was measured. The conclusion did not depend on it.)

### A parent block on all fifteen chapter pages, and 70/30 parent-facing

Chapter pages are where a reader lands to do the thing. They already carry the Reality
Check, which is the safety layer, written for the reader. Putting a parent-facing block
beside it splits the address of the page and dilutes the one element that must never be
weakened.

A quiet link from a chapter page to `/parents` is fine and does not exist yet. **That much
is adopted.** The 70/30 rebalance is not.

### Dutch, German and Spanish editions

Non-negotiable 11. Fifth review to propose it.

### The sequel franchise — and a flag nobody else would have caught

*Habits & Grow Rich*, *Deep Work For Teens*, *7 Habits Rebuilt*, *How To Win Friends
Rebuilt.* Owner's decision entirely — but one thing has to be said, because the review
presents these as the same move that worked with Hill, and they are not.

**Hill died in 1970 and *Think and Grow Rich* is 1937.** Non-negotiable 8 exists to keep the
relationship at "inspired by", and the audit is defensible because the record is public and
old.

**Clear, Newport and Epstein are living authors with current copyrights and active
trademarks.** A titled "remix" of a book published in 2018 is a different legal proposition
from a remix of one published in 1937, and *"Habits & Grow Rich"* is close enough to
*Atomic Habits* and *Think and Grow Rich* at once to attract attention from two directions.

Not a reason not to write more books. A reason not to build the franchise on other people's
titles.

### "Speed and volume" as the defence against copycats

> Verdediging: snelheid en volume. Ryan moet nu de "eerste" positie vastleggen.

This directly contradicts review 3, and **review 3 is right.** Its finding was that the moat
*is* the absence of a gap between message and practice, and that the real risk is structural
pressure to go bigger and faster. Publishing fast to hold a position is that pressure,
named as a strategy.

Worth recording that the two reviews conflict here, because both are persuasive and only one
can be acted on.
