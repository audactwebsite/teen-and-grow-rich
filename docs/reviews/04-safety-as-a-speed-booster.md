# Review 4 — "Race Control is not the brake, it is the harness"

Received 2 August 2026. Ran a parallel search and read `brightkids.ai/meet-ryan`, so its
picture of the wider ecosystem is better informed than most. Its picture of *this site* is
partly out of date.

**One genuinely new recommendation, one framing worth keeping, and one proposal that has to
be answered clearly because it will come up again.**

---

## Adopt — new

### Per-page QR codes in the second print run

Nobody else raised this, and it is the only recommendation in seven reviews aimed at the
printer rather than the site:

> Ensure the physical pages in future print runs carry page-specific QR codes that send the
> reader straight to that page's digital tool.

That is right, and it retires a workaround. `/p/1`–`/p/65` exists because the first run
shipped with two codes that both land on the root — the page-number field is a retrofit for
deep links the print could not carry. Per-page codes make the retrofit unnecessary for
anyone holding the new edition.

It does **not** make `/p/` removable. Dutch classrooms have banned phones since 1 January
2024, school Chromebooks have no camera workflow, and the first print run exists. The
number typed by hand stays.

**Recorded for the second print run**, alongside the domain fix that is already in it.

### The framing: safety as a speed booster

> In motorsport a driver learns that top brakes exist not to go slowly, but to dare to take
> corners at maximum speed. Race Control is not a restriction on the teenager's freedom. It
> is the harness that lets them build at speed without crashing.

This is the best one-line explanation of why the traffic-light system belongs to the reader
rather than to the parent — and it comes straight out of Ryan's own sport, which makes it
his rather than ours. Worth using on `/race-control`, which currently opens with a flag
metaphor that says almost the same thing less well.

### A portfolio-shaped export, distinct from the record

Measured: printing `/receipts` with data already produces a clean 2.4-page A4 record —
what is kept, builds marked done, answers written, and the honest note about browser
storage. Print produces a PDF. So "there is no PDF export" is not accurate.

But the reviewer's *use case* is different from what exists, and the distinction is fair: a
record is a checklist of what you ticked. A portfolio is a document you would attach to a
first job application or show a first client — it leads with what you built, not with how
much of the site you completed.

**Adopt as a variant**, not a replacement, and with one constraint: the moment a document
is designed to be sent to a stranger, the Reality Checks about contact routes and adult
approval apply to it. It carries the same guidance the First Offer Canvas does.

## Not adopting

### The "viral proof loop"

> The receipts are stored locally, so the proof stays isolated and no network effect or
> social proof toward the outside world can form.

This is described as a blind spot. It is a decision, and it is the one the book itself
argues for:

> *"You do not have to post your dream publicly. Accountability can be private, especially
> when safety, family, or anxiety matters. The goal is action, not exposure."*
> — Chapter 1 Reality Check

A network effect built on teenagers publishing their work is the comparison engine this
book was written against. The isolation is the product.

### Bright Kids membership or accreditation at day 90

> Once the teen finishes day 90 and has proven the basics, the logical next step is a
> membership or accreditation on BrightKids.ai.

Review 3 named this risk before this review proposed it: a subscription-and-accreditation
funnel is exactly the extractive pattern that would open the gap between message and
practice. A book that spends nineteen Reality Checks warning about paid courses promising
outcomes cannot terminate in one.

A link from the end of the 90-day path to *"here is where you can learn the skill"* is
fine. A gated tier as the reward for finishing is not.

### "Powered by" brand sponsorships

The proposal is that Canva, Notion, Raspberry Pi or a bank fund free book distribution to
schools as CSR.

**This needs a clear answer because it will come back, and the answer is no as described.**
Run it through review 3's test: could it go on the changelog without contradicting the
method? *"Chapter 5 now recommends the tools of the company paying for this"* cannot. The
site's whole claim to a school IT reviewer is that nothing here is influenced by anyone
else — and a logo on a page a thirteen-year-old reads is influence whether or not the copy
changes.

The version that survives: **a school or foundation buys books at full price and gives them
away.** That is a customer, not a sponsor. No logo, no placement, no editorial contact, and
it appears in the changelog as a sale. Same money, no gap.

### Behind-the-build content on TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn

Owner's decision, and it involves a minor appearing on camera. Not a design call.

## What this review gets right that the others missed

It is the only one to notice that Ryan's two properties should reinforce each other and to
say plainly that the book is the door and Bright Kids is the house.

Review 3 named the risk in that same relationship. Both are right, and they are the same
observation from opposite ends: the connection is valuable **only if both sides run on the
same rules.** If Bright Kids keeps a free core, sells objects rather than access, and takes
nothing from minors, the bridge is an asset. If it does not, the bridge is how the gap
reaches this site.

That is the owner's decision and it is probably the most consequential one on any of these
lists.
